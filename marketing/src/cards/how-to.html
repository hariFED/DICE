card · 01 · operator · setup HOW TO: run a DICE node
01

Unbox + plug in

Plug the device into USB-C power. Status LED blinks red → orange → white while it attaches to your WiFi. Use the DICE companion app to scan the QR on the enclosure and hand off your WiFi credentials.

02

Paste your Solana wallet

In the companion app, paste the pubkey you want rewards paid to. Your DICE-S3 signs an enrollment message and sends it to the coordinator — no private key ever leaves the chip.

03

Wait for first round

Your node appears in the /explorer mesh strip within 60 seconds. The status LED pulses white when a round is in progress. First earned SOL shows up in your wallet within ~2 minutes of your first round.

04

Optional: hold BOOT for 5s

Long-press BOOT if you ever need to re-provision (new WiFi, different wallet). The device wipes its NVS and opens a captive-portal — no re-flash required.

05

Join operator Discord

Bundled in the box: a printed invite card. Operator channel has firmware updates, incident alerts, and routing discussions.

card · 02 · developer · integrate HOW TO: use DICE in your dApp
01

Install the SDK

TypeScript: npm i @dicelabs/vrf. Rust / Anchor: cargo add dice-vrf. Both SDKs ship with Devnet + Mainnet program IDs baked in.

02

One CPI call in your program

Call request_randomness_auto from your instruction handler, passing a callback seed. The DICE program will CPI back into your chosen callback once the round finalizes.

use dice_vrf::cpi::request_randomness_auto;

request_randomness_auto(
    ctx.accounts.dice_ctx(),
    callback_seed!("roll"),
)?;
03

Handle the 32-byte result

Your callback receives DiceResult {'{'} round_id, value, proof {'}'}. Use value as a 32-byte RNG — interpret the first N bytes as a u64, or feed it into ChaCha8 for deterministic sub-streams.

04

Verify (optional)

For extra-paranoid use cases, call verify_randomness with the returned proof. This re-checks all six node signatures on-chain and costs ~8k compute units.

05

Budget 0.002 SOL per request

The DICE program deducts 0.002 SOL from the caller on request_randomness_auto. For user-facing products, either pass the cost through or absorb it (it's ~$0.30 at current prices).
