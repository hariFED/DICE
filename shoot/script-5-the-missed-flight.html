Script 5 — The Missed Flight

Style: human-first, funny cold open, real situation. The chaos IS the authenticity. Dollar Shave Club meets early Airbnb meets two guys who actually built something in their apartment.
Runtime: ~80s Energy: warm chaos → real talk → quiet conviction Arc: funny situation → "while I have you" → the real thing → callback close
Inspired by: Dollar Shave Club (humor earns the serious moment), Airbnb YC pitch (absurd origin, real product), Stripe (radical simplicity after the hook), Anatoly Yakovenko early Solana talks (builder showing what he built on his desk)

The situation 0:00

FACE — you're already in frame, slightly amused, like you just got off the phone. Casual. The white background feels almost too clean for how unpolished you're about to be.
"So."
half-beat — slight exhale, almost a laugh
"Today was supposed to be the big shoot.
Me and my cofounder Nikhil, side by side,
introducing DICE to the world."
Light. Storytelling energy. You're setting up a situation the audience can see coming.
"Nikhil missed his flight."
Deadpan. One beat of silence. Then let whatever expression comes naturally — a smile, an eye roll, a shrug. Don't plan it. The real reaction is funnier than anything you rehearse.
beat — let the audience laugh
"So... it's just me."
Gesture around at the white background, the empty space next to you.
"Me, a white wall..."
Reach down and pick up an ESP32. Hold it up.
"...and four tiny computers that I think
might fix a pretty big problem."
"pretty big problem" — understatement on purpose. You're downplaying. The audience leans in because they can tell you're about to NOT downplay.

The pivot — "while I have you" 0:18

Set the chip down. Shift. The humor was the door — now you walk them through it. Energy goes from "funny situation" to "ok but actually listen."
"But since you're here... let me tell you
why Nikhil and I spent the last few months
obsessing over random numbers."
The "but since you're here" is the pivot. It's a classic move — comedian energy into "no but seriously." The audience has already decided they like you from the flight joke. Now they'll listen to anything.
"We build lottery games on Solana.
Real money. Real players.
Someone wins thirty, forty, fifty thousand dollars
based on one random number."
half-beat
"And that number...
comes from one rented server,
run by one company,
that every game on Solana just... trusts."
Each "one" gets quieter. You're counting down to the absurdity. By the time you hit "just... trusts" your voice should be almost a murmur.
beat
"We talk about decentralization all day.
But the thing that decides where the money goes...
is a cloud rental."
No "with a blockchain receipt" this time. Just "cloud rental." Shorter. The audience fills in the rest. Less is landing harder in this script because you earned the trust with the humor.

The thing on the desk 0:38

Pick up the ESP32 again. But this time — don't hold it up dramatically. Just look at it in your palm, like it's something you carry around. Then show it to the camera casually, the way you'd show a friend something on your phone.
"This is what we built instead."
Close-up of the ESP32 in your palm. Your thumb runs across the chip markings. It looks small. It looks cheap. That's the point.
"Eight dollar chip.
Has its own cryptographic key
baked into the silicon at the factory.
Can't pull it out. Can't copy it.
Can't boot up a clone and pretend to be it."
"Can't pull it out. Can't copy it. Can't boot up a clone" — rhythm matters here. Three short swings. Like you're closing doors.
"It makes random numbers from actual physics.
Electrical noise inside the hardware.
Not math pretending to be random.
Actual noise."
Set it down. Camera widens to show all 4 devices on desk, green LEDs pulsing.
"Four of these talk to each other.
Each one commits a value nobody else can see.
Then they all reveal at the same time.
The result is a number that nobody —
not me, not Nikhil, not anyone —
could've rigged."
"not me, not Nikhil" — naming yourselves here is intentional. It says: we built it, and even WE can't cheat it. That's the trust model in human language.

The receipts 0:58

Quick split screen. Left: two transaction blocks, timer counting 5... 6... 7... Right: one block, 3.5 seconds, checkmark. 3 seconds total. No labels. Obvious.
"One Solana transaction. Three and a half seconds.
Ten lines of code to integrate."
Fast. You're stacking proof now, not telling a story. The audience is already sold on WHY — now you're showing them it actually works.
"And we're shipping something
nobody else on Solana has —
streaming randomness.
You subscribe once. Values keep flowing."
River of glowing values flowing from the 4 devices into a game interface. Continuous. Alive.

The callback 1:08

Slight smile comes back. The warmth from the opening returns. You're closing the loop.
"Look — Nikhil's gonna land eventually."
Warm. The audience remembers the opening. They smile. You've got them.
"And when he does, he's gonna find out
we did the whole launch without him."
half-beat — let them laugh
Drop the smile. Look right into the camera. The shift from warm to serious in 1 second is what makes this ending hit.
"But what he's also gonna find...
is that the games we've been building together
are actually fair now."
beat
"For real this time."
"For real this time" — four words that carry the weight of everything. It implies: before, it wasn't real. Before, we were trusting something we shouldn't have. Now we're not. Said quiet. Said like a promise to a friend, not a pitch to a judge. This is the line Nikhil hears when he watches this on the plane WiFi.
hold 2 seconds — cut
Black screen. 1 second. Then white. DICE logo, clean. Below: "Real hardware. Real entropy. Solana." Smaller: your URL. Even smaller, in grey, almost hidden: "Nikhil, check your phone." Hold 4 seconds. Fade.
The "Nikhil, check your phone" easter egg on the end card. Tiny. Most viewers won't even notice it. But the ones who do will screenshot it and share it. That's how things go viral — not from the main message, but from the human detail people discover and feel clever for finding.
