Script 2 — The Eight Dollar Chip

Style: product-first — opens with the object, not the problem. Steve Jobs "one more thing" energy but scrappy.
Runtime: ~75s Energy: medium, builds Arc: object → context → "why this matters"

The object 0:00

OPEN ON: your hand, holding the ESP32 against the white background. Face not visible yet. Just the chip, filling the frame.
"This costs eight dollars."
beat — camera holds on the chip
Camera slowly pulls back to reveal your face behind it.
"It has a cryptographic key baked into the silicon
that nobody — not me, not you, not the manufacturer —
can ever take out."
Hold the chip steady. Don't wave it around. The stillness of the object contrasts with the weight of what you're saying about it.
beat
"And it makes random numbers
from actual physical noise inside the hardware."
"Not math pretending to be random.
Actual... noise. Heat. Electrons. Physics."
"Actual... noise" — the pause before noise is you searching for the right word in real-time. Don't rehearse it smooth. The stumble makes it human.

Why you should care 0:18

Set the chip down. Lean forward. Now it's about the world, not the object.
"Here's why that matters."
"On Solana right now... there are games,
lotteries, raffles, NFT mints...
where real money — sometimes tens of thousands —
gets decided by a random number."
"And that number comes from a cloud server."
beat
"A single. Rented. Server."
Punch each word. "Single." [beat] "Rented." [beat] "Server." Three sentences, not one. Let each one land separately.
"I know because I build these games.
I ship lotteries for a living.
And I was trusting someone else's computer
to be the most important part of my product."
The "I was trusting" admission — let the discomfort show. You're not proud of this. That's what makes you credible.

The network 0:38

Camera widens. Your desk visible. 4 ESP32s with green LEDs. You gesture toward them.
"So now there's four of these on my desk."
"Each one generates its own entropy.
Each one commits before seeing anyone else's.
And the final number is a combination
that no single device could've rigged."
Simple animation: 4 nodes, each producing a glowing value independently. Values flow to center, combine, produce one output. Arrows show the commit-reveal timing — locked in, then revealed. Clean, not flashy.
"No cloud. No enclave.
No company you have to trust
to not peek at the answer early."

The speed 0:55

"And it's fast."
Single transaction block appears. Timer: 3.5 seconds. Done. Checkmark. Below in small text: "vs. 2 transactions, 3-8 seconds (Switchboard)" — but muted, not the focus.
"One Solana transaction.
Commit, reveal, finalize — all in one shot.
Three and a half seconds."
"And we're building streaming —
where you subscribe once
and randomness just... flows to you."
"just... flows" — open your hand palm-up when you say this, like releasing water. Let the gesture paint the idea.

The close 1:08

Pick up one of the ESP32s again. Hold it in front of you. Look at it, then at the camera.
"Eight dollars."
beat
"This is what fair looks like."
Bookend with the opening. "Eight dollars" was the first thing you said and the second-to-last. The loop closes. "This is what fair looks like" is the thesis of the entire video in six words. Hold eye contact. No smile. Cut.
cut to black
DICE logo on white. "Real hardware. Real entropy. Solana." URL. Hold 3 seconds.
