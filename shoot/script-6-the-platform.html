Script 6 — The Platform

Style: professional product launch. Opens with human story, reveals VRF as the first act, then zooms out to show the bigger platform play. This is the "we're not a feature, we're infrastructure" video. Investors and judges watch this one twice.
Runtime: ~100s Energy: grounded confidence, escalating scope Arc: our story → the first product → "but that's just the beginning" → the platform
Inspired by: Stripe Atlas launch (start small, reveal the empire), Helium's evolution pitch (LoRa → 5G → WiFi, same device), Solana's "why this chain" framing

Who we are — fast 0:00

FACE — already in frame. Grounded. Not smiling yet. You know what you're about to say and you've earned the right to say it.
"I'm Hari. My cofounder Nikhil and I
build lottery games on Solana."
"The kind where someone puts in real money...
and one random number decides who walks out with it."
Four lines. Names, what you do, what's at stake. Done in under 8 seconds. Don't linger.

The problem — 10 seconds, not a speech 0:08

"That random number?
On every VRF on Solana right now...
it comes from a cloud server.
One company. One machine."
beat
"We thought we could do better."
No long exposition. No "we built an industry on a promise." This script is PROFESSIONAL — state the problem, move to the solution. The audience is here for what you built, not what's broken.

DICE — the first product 0:18

Pick up the ESP32. Hold it casually, at chest height.
"This is DICE."
"An eight dollar microcontroller
with a cryptographic key burned into the chip."
Close-up of the ESP32-S3 in your hand. Macro shot of the chip surface. The "8 dollars" price point should feel tangible — this is small, cheap, real.
"It generates randomness from physical entropy —
electrical noise inside the hardware —
and signs it with a key that can never be extracted."
Set it down next to the other 3 devices. Camera widens to show all 4, green LEDs.
"Four of these run a commit-reveal protocol.
Each one locks in a secret value.
Nobody sees anyone else's.
They reveal simultaneously.
The output is a number nobody could've gamed."
Simple animation: 4 nodes → 4 sealed values → simultaneous reveal → merge → one golden output number. 4 seconds. Clean.
"One Solana transaction. Three and a half seconds.
Ten lines of code to integrate.
First streaming VRF on Solana —
subscribe once, randomness flows continuously."
Stack the receipts fast. One TX. 3.5s. 10 LOC. Streaming. Four punches, no pauses between them. The speed communicates confidence.

The turn — "but here's what most people miss" 0:42

This is the moment the video stops being a VRF pitch and becomes a platform story. Energy shifts. You lean forward slightly. The audience thinks they've seen the product — you're about to show them they've only seen the first layer.
beat — let the VRF section breathe for 1 second
"But here's the thing most people miss."
half-beat
"VRF... is just what this chip does first."
THIS is the pivot line. "Just what this chip does FIRST." The word "first" changes everything. Suddenly the audience recalculates — wait, there's more? The device is a platform?
Pick up the ESP32 again. But now you're looking at it differently — not as a VRF device, but as a platform.
"This chip has Bluetooth.
It has forty open GPIO pins.
It has hardware crypto that signs anything.
It sleeps on ten microamps of power.
And it costs less than your coffee."
"costs less than your coffee" — throw it away casually. Don't sell it. The understatement does the selling.

The platform reveal 0:55

"Same device. Same key. Same Solana program.
But now imagine what else it can do."
The single ESP32 in the center of the screen. One by one, service labels appear around it in a circle, each one lighting up as you say it: → "VRF" (already glowing — this is what you launched) → "Location Proof" (lights up) → "Sensor Attestation" (lights up) → "Hardware Signing" (lights up) The visual message: one device, expanding capabilities. Like a skill tree unlocking.
"Proof of location.
BLE and LoRa triangulation —
GPS-independent, hardware-signed,
posted on-chain.
Supply chain tracking. Insurance verification.
Proving a DePIN node is actually where it claims to be."
Don't list use cases like a slide deck. Say them like you're brainstorming out loud — "supply chain... insurance... DePIN verification..." — each one is a door you're opening and the audience is peeking through.
"Sensor attestation.
Plug a two dollar temperature sensor into this board
and suddenly you have hardware-signed weather data
that a cloud server literally cannot produce."
"literally cannot produce" — lean on "literally." It's not an exaggeration. A cloud VM does not have a temperature sensor. The hardware advantage isn't marketing here — it's physics.
"Hardware signing.
A five dollar distributed HSM.
DAOs paying fifty thousand for a hardware security module...
could use a network of these instead."
The $5 vs $50K contrast is the number that makes investors sit up. Don't rush past it. Let the math register.

The Solana DePIN moment 1:18

Clean data slide, Apple keynote style: "Solana DePIN" at top. Three numbers fade in one at a time: "$3.25B market cap" "5.7M+ devices deployed" "No other chain comes close." Hold 3 seconds.
"Solana owns DePIN.
Three and a quarter billion in market cap.
Five point seven million devices worldwide.
No other chain is even close."
"But most of those devices do one thing.
Helium does connectivity. Hivemapper does maps.
Render does compute."
beat
"DICE does cryptographic services.
Starting with randomness.
Expanding to location, sensors, signing.
One node. Multiple services. Same eight dollars."
"One node. Multiple services. Same eight dollars." — this is the thesis of the entire platform compressed into one line. It's the Helium evolution story (LoRa → 5G → WiFi, same hotspot) applied to crypto services. If an investor remembers one sentence from this video, it should be this one.

The close 1:35

No device in hand. No motion graphics. Just you. The platform vision has been painted — now ground it in something personal.
"Nikhil and I started this because our games
deserved better randomness."
half-beat
"Turns out... Solana deserves
better infrastructure."
The callback to the personal story. "Our games → Solana." Small to big. The scope has expanded in 90 seconds from "our lottery app" to "Solana's DePIN infrastructure layer." But it doesn't feel like scope creep because every step was earned — VRF is live, the chip is real, the expansion is logical.
hold 2 seconds — cut
Black. 1 second. White screen. DICE logo, clean and centered. Below it, four words in a row, spaced evenly: "Randomness. Location. Sensors. Signing." The word "Randomness" is white (shipped). The other three are grey (coming). URL below. "DePIN for cryptographic services." Hold 4 seconds. Fade.
The end card does quiet storytelling: "Randomness" in white = this is live, this is real. The three grey words = this is where we're going. Investors see a roadmap without you having to put up a timeline slide. Judges see ambition grounded in execution.
