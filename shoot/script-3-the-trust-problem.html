Script 3 — The Trust Problem

Style: philosophical opener — starts with a big idea about trust in crypto, narrows to VRF, then introduces the fix. TED-talk pacing.
Runtime: ~75s Energy: thoughtful, building to conviction Arc: big idea → specific failure → solution → vision

The big idea 0:00

FACE — medium, centered, calm. Speaking like the opening line of a talk. Measured.
"We built an entire industry on one promise."
beat
"Don't trust. Verify."
Pause a full second after "verify." This line is sacred in crypto. You're about to break it. The audience needs to feel it's solid before you crack it.
"Billions of dollars move on Solana
without anyone trusting a bank, a broker, a middleman.
That's the whole point."
beat
"But there's one place where we still just... trust."
"just... trust" — the ellipsis is real. You're choosing the word carefully, almost reluctantly. Like admitting something the community doesn't want to hear.

Who we are + what we found 0:12

FACE — quick, grounded. Names + context in one breath, then straight into the problem.
"I'm Hari, this is Nikhil. We build lottery games on Solana
and we built DICE — because we found something
that we couldn't un-see."
Names, product, and hook in 5 seconds. "DICE" lands before the 20-second mark. "Couldn't un-see" creates curiosity — what did they find?
"The random number that picks who wins the pot...
comes from a rented cloud server.
One company. One machine.
And every game on Solana just... trusts it."
beat
"We'd shipped five games on top of this.
Never questioned it."
The admission. Quick, honest. The audience thinks "wait, I haven't checked either."
"That kept us up."
Three words. Don't elaborate.

The fix 0:30

Energy lifts. You reach for the ESP32. Hold it up. The camera should see the chip clearly.
"So we built the thing that should've existed."
hold the device — 2 seconds of nothing
"DICE.
Decentralized Infrastructure for Cryptographic Entropy."
First and only time you say the full name. It should sound deliberate, like each word was chosen. Because they were. The "so we built" connects your founder story to the product — you didn't find DICE, you made it because you had to.
"A network of these — eight dollar microcontrollers —
each with a key that lives and dies in the silicon.
They generate randomness from hardware noise,
commit their answers independently,
and reveal them simultaneously."
"The output is a number that no single device,
no single person, no single company
could have predicted or controlled."
4 nodes on a map. Each produces a sealed value (lock icon). All locks open at once. Values merge into one glowing number in the center. Clean, geometric, satisfying.

The proof 0:50

"It lands on Solana in one transaction.
Three and a half seconds."
"Ten lines of code to plug into your game."
"And we're building streaming VRF —
the first on Solana —
where random values flow to your contract
continuously. No request. No wait. Just... there."

The vision 1:02

No device in hand anymore. Just you. Looking at the camera like this is the reason you and Nikhil started the whole thing.
"We said 'don't trust, verify.'
Nikhil and I are building the part
where we actually mean it."
2 seconds — hold, then cut
Callback to the opener. The audience remembers "don't trust, verify" from 0:03. Hearing it again at the end, with "I think it's time we meant it," closes the loop. The implication: we haven't been meaning it. Until now. Don't explain that. Let the audience feel it.
DICE logo. White background. "DePIN for cryptographic services." URL. 3 seconds. Fade.
