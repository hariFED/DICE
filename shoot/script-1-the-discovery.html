Script 1 — The Discovery

Style: quiet investigative — like a short documentary where the founder narrates what he found
Runtime: ~80s Energy: low-to-medium Arc: mystery → reveal → build

The question 0:00

FACE — medium shot, looking slightly past camera like remembering something, then snapping to lens
"I asked a really simple question one night."
beat
"Where does the random number come from?"
Say it like you're genuinely still curious. Not rhetorical. Like you asked it to your screen at midnight and nobody answered.
"I build lottery games on Solana.
People put in real money... and one number decides who gets it.
I'd shipped five of these.
Never once checked where the number actually came from."
The "never once checked" should sting a little. You're admitting something. That honesty is what makes the audience trust you.

The answer nobody likes 0:18

"So I looked."
2 seconds — hold eye contact, say nothing
"It's a server.
One server. In a data center. Run by one company."
Single server rack in darkness. A thin bright line connects it to a cluster of dApp icons — lottery wheels, dice, NFT mints. All dependent on this one box. The image should feel fragile.
"Every lottery. Every coin flip.
Every NFT that picks which trait you get."
beat
"All of it comes from one rented machine
that I have to just... believe is being honest."
The word "believe" should feel uncomfortable in your mouth. You're a builder — you don't do belief, you do verification. That tension is the scene.
silence — 2 seconds
"That's not decentralized.
That's a cloud rental with a blockchain receipt."
THE LINE. Flat. Tired. Like you've said it 50 times to yourself already. No performance. Just the fact.

What I did about it 0:40

HARD CUT — energy shift. You reach below frame and bring up the ESP32. Hold it at chest height.
"So I went and built the thing that should've existed."
hold the device — let camera see it — 2 seconds of nothing
"This is an eight dollar chip.
It has its own key — burned into the silicon.
Can't copy it. Can't extract it.
Can't snapshot it and roll back like a cloud VM."
Tap the chip gently when you say "burned into the silicon." The tactile moment makes it real.
Set the device down. Camera widens slightly to show 4 devices on desk, LEDs green.
"Four of these generate their own randomness.
Each one locks in before seeing anyone else's answer.
And the final number comes from all of them combined."
"Not from one server.
Not from one company.
From physics."
The three "not from" lines should feel like doors closing. Then "from physics" is the one that opens.

What it means for you 1:00

Split screen — left: two transaction blocks appearing slowly with a timer counting to 7 seconds. Right: ONE block, timer stops at 3.5s, green checkmark. Clean. No labels. The visual is self-explanatory.
"One transaction. Three and a half seconds. Done.
Ten lines of code to integrate."
"And something nobody on Solana has shipped yet —
streaming randomness.
You subscribe once... and values just keep coming."
Continuous river of glowing values flowing from the devices into a game UI. Feels alive, continuous, effortless.

The close 1:10

Pull back. Calm. Looking right into the lens. Hands still.
"If someone's money is on the line...
the number that decides it
should come from the real world."
2 seconds — hold
"Not from a rental."
Last word. Don't nod. Don't smile. Don't add "check us out" or "visit our site." Just... stop. The silence after "rental" IS the ending.
cut to black — 1s
White screen. DICE logo centered. Below it: "Real hardware. Real entropy. Solana." URL beneath. Hold 3 seconds. Fade.
