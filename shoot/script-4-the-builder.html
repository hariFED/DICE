Script 4 — The Builder

Style: raw workshop energy — you're at your desk, devices around you, showing what you built like a friend walked in and said "what's all this?" Least polished, most authentic.
Runtime: ~70s Energy: casual, escalating pride Arc: "let me show you" → the problem in 10 words → the build → the moment it worked

The desk 0:00

OPEN ON: your desk. 4 ESP32 devices scattered. Wires. Laptop open with serial monitor scrolling. Your hands enter frame picking up a device. Camera tilts up to your face. You're mid-thought, like the camera interrupted you working.
"Ok so — this is gonna sound weird."
Throwaway energy. Like texting a voice note to a friend. NOT a prepared opening. The "ok so" is the signal to the audience that this isn't rehearsed.
"I spent the last few months
building tiny computers that make random numbers."
half-beat — slight smile
"And I think it might actually matter."
The smile is self-aware. You know it sounds absurd. The "I think it might actually matter" is the hook — WHY would random numbers from tiny computers matter? The audience stays to find out.

The 10-word problem 0:10

"Here's the thing.
I build games on Solana. Lotteries, mostly.
The random number that picks the winner?
Comes from someone else's cloud server."
Fast. Don't dwell. You're stating a fact you've already processed, not discovering it in real-time. The audience catches up to you, not the other way around.
"One server. Not decentralized. Just... rented."
"And I thought —
if I don't trust a bank to hold my money...
why am I trusting a random cloud box
to decide who gets it?"
The bank analogy is the bridge for non-technical viewers. Every crypto person understands "I don't trust banks." Connecting that to "so why do I trust this cloud server" makes the problem click instantly.

The build 0:25

You hold up one ESP32 between your fingers. Close enough the camera can see the chip markings.
"So. This is an ESP32.
Eight dollar microcontroller."
"I put a key in it that can't come out.
It makes randomness from its own hardware —
electrical noise, thermal jitter,
stuff that's physically impossible to predict."
You set it down, gesture to all 4 on the desk.
"Then I made four of them talk to each other."
Quick animation: 4 small nodes, each produces a sealed envelope. All envelopes open simultaneously. Contents merge. One golden number emerges. Total duration: 4 seconds. No labels needed.
"They each commit a secret value.
Nobody sees anyone else's.
Then they all reveal at once.
The result is a number nobody could've gamed."

The moment it worked 0:45

You glance at your laptop screen — the serial monitor is visible, logs scrolling. This is real. Not staged.
"First time all four hit at once...
commit, reveal, finalize —
one Solana transaction, three and a half seconds..."
beat — you look at camera
"I just sat there staring at the screen."
This is a real memory. If it actually happened, let the feeling come back while you say it. If you embellish, the audience will feel it. If you're honest, they'll feel that too. Honesty wins.
"Because this little eight dollar thing
just did what a fifty thousand dollar cloud setup does.
Except nobody can tamper with it.
And nobody needs to trust anyone."

What's next 0:58

"Right now we're building streaming VRF —
where a game subscribes once
and random values just keep arriving.
No one else on Solana does that."
"Ten lines to integrate.
And the whole thing runs on hardware
that costs less than lunch."
"costs less than lunch" — land this casually. It's a punchline that works because you don't sell it. You just say it and move on.

The close 1:08

You pick up one device one last time. Look at it in your hand. Then at the camera.
"I don't know if this changes everything.
But I know that the games I build now...
are actually fair."
beat
"And I can prove it."
The vulnerability of "I don't know if this changes everything" followed by the quiet confidence of "and I can prove it" — that's the emotional range in 3 seconds. Don't rush between them. Let the doubt sit, then let the certainty replace it.
hold 2 seconds — cut
DICE logo on white. "DePIN for cryptographic services." URL. 3 seconds.
